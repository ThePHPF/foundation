## Discussion Details

- **Board Membership Change:** Roman Pronskiy announced that they are stepping down from the Board and proposed Brent Roose as the new representative for JetBrains. The Board reached a consensus through a unanimous quorum vote to approve Brent, and Elizabeth Barron committed to handling the onboarding, website updates, and public announcement.

- **Annual Application Process:** Elizabeth Barron initially proposed skipping the open application process to prioritize raises for existing contractors; however, Nils Adermann argued that keeping applications open is necessary to find new ideas and talent. The Board reached a consensus to open applications while clearly communicating that they are unlikely to expand the team size without increased funding, framing the process as a way to maintain current high-quality contributions. Roman Pronskiy noted the historical success of this process, mentioning that consistent applicants were eventually hired when a suitable role emerged.

- **European Currency and Contractor Payments:** Elizabeth Barron highlighted the need for the foundation to pay European contractors in Euros, which requires directing more sponsor funds to the Euro-based Open Collective. While Nils Adermann noted that Open Collective currently lacks the technical ability to fully merge the US dollar and Euro ledgers, the Board decided to proceed by promoting the Euro Open Collective page and EUR sponsorship tiers, while maintaining stable, annual pay rates for contractors. The EUR rate will be determined yearly, based on the exchange rates in effect on January 1 of that year.

- **Sovereign Tech Fund and Grant Strategy:** The current Sovereign Tech Fund (STF) project is scheduled to end in September, and Sebastian and Nils Adermann discussed the challenges of securing a third grant, as STF typically aims to avoid recurring dependency. Elizabeth Barron committed to conducting an audit of available grant institutions, including NLnet and others, to explore future funding opportunities.

- **FrOScon Booth Feedback:** Sebastian reported on the booth experience at FrOScon, noting that while there were few technical questions/requests for the language, there was high interest in the Foundation's governance over the PHP language and existence. Sebastian identified a lack of marketing materials, specifically QR codes for donations and take-away flyers for office decision-makers, which Matt Stauffer confirmed can be produced for future events.

- **Public Sector PHP Awareness:** Sebastian presented research regarding PHP's significant footprint in European public sector projects, such as those within the European Commission and German government. The Board discussed turning this research into an article or conference presentation to raise awareness, and Matt Stauffer noted that integrating this messaging into the php.net website would help advocate for PHP in enterprise and government environments.

- **Podcast Production:** Elizabeth Barron provided an update on the PHP at Scale podcast, noting that Ben has already recorded an interview with PassCreator and is coordinating further episodes. The current plan is to edit and accumulate three to five recorded episodes before the official launch.

- **All Things Open Conference:** Elizabeth Barron indicated that there is booth space available at the All Things Open conference and is currently researching cost-effective options, such as community tables, to maintain a presence. This is a more interesting endeavor given the feedback from FrOScon. 

- **Elephpant Fundraising Proposal:** Josepha Haden Chomphosy and Sara expressed strong reservations about the proposed elephpant fundraising campaign, citing high administrative costs, a lack of potential for profit, and concerns that the model resembles a "challenge coin" system that is not suitable for the project. Nils Adermann argued that the elephant fundraising initiative was ineffective because it targeted individuals already familiar with the organization, making the effort pointless regarding awareness. Sara contended that the initiative served as a second-degree amplifier that could help build community and show support for other projects. Elizabeth Barron clarified that the intent, originally proposed by Damien and expanded by Ben Marks, was to foster solidarity and offer support to other projects and extensions struggling with funding, rather than serving solely as a foundation fundraiser. Josepha Haden Chomphosy expressed that the initiative was only incrementally beneficial. Nils Adermann suggested that fundraising efforts should prioritize engaging larger corporations that profit from the PHP ecosystem rather than targeting individual donors. Matt Stauffer and Anne McCarthy voiced concerns that individuals might feel they had fulfilled their contribution duties through small elephant purchases, even though these efforts did not result in significant financial support. Elizabeth Barron acknowledged these perspectives and with Board consensus decided that they would not proceed with the elephant fundraiser.

- **Team Updates:** As most of the meeting was dedicated to discussion, an overview of team updates were briefly provided. Elizabeth Barron encouraged Board members to read through the updates in the agenda at their convenience.
