# Travel Reimbursement Policy - The PHP Foundation


## Overview

This Travel and Reimbursable Expense Policy (“Policy”) has been created to memorialize The PHP Foundation’s (“Foundation”) reimbursement policies relating to travel and other business expenses incurred by Foundation-approved contractors and staff while engaged in business on behalf of, or at the behest of Foundation (“Travelers”). For purposes of this policy, approval from “Foundation” means approval from the ED or a Board member.


## Purpose

Foundation must maintain effective control of business-related expenses in order to maintain its financial viability. Foundation is also accountable to our donors to ensure that we manage their contributions wisely and maximize our ability to pursue our mission. As such, Foundation expects Travelers to use good judgment and to claim reimbursement for only those expenses that are necessary and reasonable. Excessive expenses, including but not limited to luxury accommodations and services unnecessary for, or unrelated to the furtherance of Foundation’s mission, are not eligible for reimbursement.

Any travel expense that adheres to this Policy is considered In-Policy and does not require special approval, so long as the trip itself has been approved in writing by the Executive Director. 


## Travel Guidelines

Travelers should adhere to the following guidelines to stay In-Policy.


### Nearby events

If you are attending an event that is nearby to where you live (accessible by local transportation), you may claim Meals and Incidental Expenses (M&IE) for any day of attendance at the travel day rate.

Please also consider if your destination may be reasonably reached by train, rather than by plane for environmental reasons.


### Flights

#### Class of Service

Coach and/or Economy Airfare is the only acceptable class for all flights (domestic and international) unless a Traveler provides a special exception and a valid reason (such as a need for business class due to a documented medical reason) to Foundation for written approval. Travelers may select their airline of choice (e.g., for the purpose of collecting airline miles and rewards), provided that the resulting fare otherwise meets the requirements of this Policy. Travelers should not book out-of-Policy trips (and thus pay a higher fare) in order to qualify for a mileage upgrade.

#### Advance Purchase

Air travel should be booked at least 14 days in advance; any travel booked less than 14 days in advance requires written pre-authorization by Foundation. Flights beyond 365 days in advance also require written pre-authorization by Foundation.

#### Low Fare

Foundation aims to balance cost savings with time savings and convenience. Budgets for flights are set based on their travel time compared to the flight with the lowest available fare. Flights with fares that are within budget are in-Policy.

If you want to upgrade your flight at your own expense, you can do so without prior approval. You will not be reimbursed for the additional amount.

Travelers may book their tickets on different dates or use any site as long as they used a fare search site to determine that the booked flights are within Policy.

#### Reasonable Flights

Foundation asks that Travelers allow for flexibility with respect to departure times during a desired day of travel, as well as longer trips in order to reduce cost. However, Foundation does consider flights with two or more connections to be unreasonable and does not expect Travelers to consider those flight options when booking travel.

It is permissible to adjust the times in your fare search to only reasonable travel times (i.e. avoiding red-eyes where possible, avoiding very early morning or late at night flights or if you need certain times due to obligations, personal or professional, you cannot move). If the cheapest flight includes a layover of more than 3 hours more than the other relevant flights, or an overnight layover where one is not necessary, you can simply ignore the cheaper inconvenient flight (and say so in your reimbursement request).

#### Excess Baggage

Should a Traveler travel on an airline that charges for a single piece of checked baggage, such a baggage expense is eligible for reimbursement with a receipt. Travelers are responsible for charges on any baggage beyond a single piece, unless that additional baggage is materials specifically related to the Foundation’s mission (i.e., bringing t-shirts and other promotional materials to an event).

#### Out-of-Policy Bookings

All air travel not adhering to the above Policies is considered Out-of-Policy and requires written pre-authorization by Foundation.

#### Cancellation Fees

Cancellation fees and other penalties incurred as a result of a change of plans are reimbursable at Foundation’s discretion. In general, Foundation shall reimburse such fees if the Traveler can submit a valid reason for the change of plans. Acceptable reasons include Foundation and/or the carrier canceling or altering the trip or unexpected delays in flight connections. In instances where these fees are incurred without adequate explanation, Foundation reserves the right to refuse to reimburse the cost of the fees.


### Other Transportation

#### Ground Transportation

Ground transportation necessary as part of authorized trips is considered to be a reasonable expense. Public ground transportation, such as taxis, shuttles, buses and municipal transit, are generally the most cost-effective options and are the standard for eligible ground transportation reimbursements. The rental of compact cars is encouraged; mid-size vehicles are authorized when necessary (e.g., when compact-sized vehicles are not available, the number of passengers requires a larger car, physical limitations of the Traveler, or volume of baggage makes a compact vehicle impractical). Rideshare services (such as Uber and Lyft) may be used if personal safety is a concern or if there is another reasonable circumstance. 

#### Rail Transportation

Rail transportation as a means of travel for an authorized Foundation trip is considered to be a reasonable expense. All rail transportation must be in economy and/or coach class.

#### Use of Personal Vehicles

When circumstances require Travelers to utilize their personal vehicles for Foundation purposes, they can be reimbursed at the current USA IRS Standard Mileage Rate[3], plus any related parking expenses and toll fees. Drivers are encouraged to find the lowest cost parking area reasonably near their destination.


### Hotels and Lodging

You may stay at the official conference hotel or any hotel that is the same price or less. Where there is no conference hotel, you may book a hotel at the US Department of State rate +20%. See the “Rates” section below for details.

Lodging documentation submitted as part of a reimbursement request must include a copy of the hotel invoice detailing all charges (credit card receipts alone are unacceptable). In particular, since Foundation only reimburses for room charges (plus relevant taxes and fees) for the necessary travel dates, the receipt from the hotel must clearly show the dates of stay, and separately list room charges and any food or service charges. Foundation will not reimburse Travelers for any costs associated with an upgrade of room accommodations.

Be sure the receipt from your hotel includes the dates of your stay, not just a total.


### Meals and Incidentals

Foundation will reimburse Traveler for meals and incidental expenses up to the M&IE per diem rate mentioned under the “Rates” section below. Because this limit varies based on the city you are traveling to, it is important to be aware of this rate before you travel. Receipts will be required for every meal and incidental expense. 

### Provided Meals

When you are able to eat a meal at no cost to you (free breakfast at a hotel, lunch at a conference, dinner is paid for by someone else, etc.), please reduce your daily M&IE limit by the appropriate amount:

* Breakfast: 25% of the rate
* Lunch: 25% of the rate
* Dinner: 50% of the rate

If a meal is offered that does not align with your dietary restrictions, you should feel free to purchase a replacement meal under the M&IE limit as usual.

#### Group Meals

For groups of Travelers on an In-Policy trip, each Traveler should pay for his/her own meals, seeing as all participants will have an opportunity to submit for separate reimbursements after the trip. Travelers anticipating a need to cover a large group meal should seek pre-approval from Foundation for such expenses before the trip.

For any such group meal, Foundation will require a written paragraph summary of the meeting, indicating what was accomplished for the Foundation.

#### Meals For Organizational Development

Travelers may occasionally have the need to invite third parties (e.g., prospective donors, contributors, community members, etc.) to meals in order to further the Foundation’s mission. Foundation recommends that Travelers seek pre-approval from the ED for such meals.

For any such organizational development meal, Foundation will require a written paragraph summary of the meeting, indicating what was accomplished for the Foundation’s mission.


### Other Expenses

#### Conference Registration Fees

Foundation will reimburse conference registration fees as long as expenses have been pre-approved by ED. 

#### Internet Access

Internet access/Wi-Fi fees charged by a hotel are reimbursable, provided that they are listed on the hotel/lodging invoice submitted for reimbursement. Other internet access fees (e.g., airport internet services, personal Wi-Fi hotspots, internet cafes) are reimbursable if they fall within the allotted maximum daily expense for M&IE.

#### Visa Fees

Foundation will reimburse fees charged by an embassy (or any government agency) that are mandatory for receiving a visa (or other permission) to enter the final destination country of travel, provided proper documentation of cost (such as a receipt, or a copy of the visa along with government published rates).

Foundation may reimburse other ancillary costs, such as use of a travel expert agency for obtaining a visa, or additional domestic travel costs necessary to obtain a visa, but all such other costs require prior approval from Foundation.

#### Non-reimbursable Expenses

Non-reimbursable expenses are identified throughout this policy. The following items are typically non-reimbursable expenses:

* Partner, spouse, and/or companion travel
* First class travel (unless medically necessary)
* Upgrades to air travel, car rentals, or hotel rooms
* Purchase of clothing, luggage, toiletries and other miscellaneous personal items
* Supplemental travel or car rental insurance
* Fines, penalties, or legal fees
* Personal entertainment or recreational expenses

Travelers are permitted to pay for their own upgrades, or use bonus programs to upgrade Foundation-reimbursed expenses. However, Travelers must ensure that Foundation does not receive nor reimburse any charges for any such transaction.


### Receipts

Keep and submit PDFs of the following, as applicable:

* Your flight receipt
* Your ground transportation receipts: car rental, taxi service, and/or rail tickets (e-confirmation of purchase is sufficient)
* Your hotel/lodging invoice
* Your meals and other incidental expenses, such as Internet access
* Your conference ticket
* Any other covered expense

_All reimbursable expenses will require an accompanying receipt._


### Reimbursement 

#### Expense Reporting

Foundation uses Open Collective as the platform to manage travel reimbursement. Travelers seeking reimbursement must submit an expense report to Open Collective with the following information, and in accordance with the Open Collective Reimbursement Policy[4]:

* Name of Traveler
* Brief description of trip and trip’s purpose (e.g., “August 2026 trip to XYZ conference for ABC project, served as planning committee member”)
* A brief paragraph explaining what was accomplished for the project during your travel.
* All associated expenses with accompanying receipts (as outlined above)
* Mention of expenses that were covered elsewhere

In the event that it is impractical to obtain a required receipt and/or if such receipt has been inadvertently destroyed or lost, the Traveler should furnish a written statement to that effect, as well as an explanation of the expenditure involved. When possible, secondary documentation (such as a redacted credit card bill) should be provided instead of the lost/destroyed receipt.

Any expense without a substantiated receipt and/or a supporting written statement will not be reimbursed. 

Foundation requests that all expense reports be submitted **within four weeks of travel**. Expense reports filed more than 90 days after the last day of travel (or for other reimbursable expenses, the day expenses are incurred) will not be reimbursed.

#### Satisfaction of IRS Requirements

For United States citizens, reimbursed travel expenses are subject to examination by the USA Internal Revenue Service (IRS). Travelers are responsible for retaining documentary evidence that all expenses are strictly for Foundation-related purposes, not personal in nature, and therefore not includable as taxable income to the Traveler. Receipts are required for all expenses, no matter the amount.


### Rates

Throughout this document, we refer to rates reported by other parties.

For travel in the United States, we follow the maximum rates for lodging and M&IE per diem set by the US General Services Administration[1].

For travel outside the United States, we follow the maximum rates for lodging and M&IE per diem set by the US Department of State [2].

We calculate the total per diem allowance for a trip using the same method as the GSA. Travelers may expense M&IE up to 100% of the listed rate for each full day of travel, plus 75% of the listed rate for each partial day of travel. For example, if you fly to a conference on Monday, spend Tuesday through Thursday at the conference, and return home on Friday, and the maximum reimbursable M&IE rate for the conference city is $80, you may request reimbursement up to $360: $80 for each day Tuesday through Thursday, plus $60 for each day you flew.


### Limitations and Budget

The Executive Director (“ED”) is responsible for monitoring the available balance of any funds budgeted for Travel and Expenses and for granting or refusing approval for travel expense requests based on an assessment of the funds available and of any outstanding contracts payable.  The ED is not to approve travel expense requests when there are not sufficient funds to cover the expense. 


### Policy Changes

The Foundation reserves the right to change any terms of this Policy from time to time. The Policy of record shall be the Policy most recently distributed by the Foundation.
